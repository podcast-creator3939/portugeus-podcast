### Expressão do dia: *botar a mão na massa*

- **Meaning:** to roll up your sleeves and actually do the work; to do something hands-on instead of just talking or planning. *Colocar a mão na massa* means the same.
- **Literal translation:** "to put your hand in the dough."
- **More examples:**
  - *Chega de reunião! Vamos botar a mão na massa e terminar esse projeto hoje.* — Enough meetings! Let's get to work and finish this project today.
  - *No curso de culinária, os alunos botam a mão na massa desde a primeira aula.* — In the cooking course, students get hands-on from the very first class.

### Ponto de gramática: the passive voice with *ser* + past participle

Brazilian Portuguese forms the passive with **ser** + **past participle**. The participle agrees in gender and number with the subject:

- "O pacote ia **ser votado** na Câmara." (o pacote → votad**o**)
- "As propostas de mudança **foram rejeitadas**." (as propostas → rejeitad**as**)
- "Setenta vestidos por mês **são vendidos** pra todo o Brasil." (os vestidos → vendid**os**)
- "A semana **é organizada** pelo Ministério." (a semana → organizad**a**)

The agent (who does the action) is introduced with **por** (+ article → **pelo / pela / pelos / pelas**): *O livro foi escrito **pela** professora.* — The book was written by the teacher.

Tip: in everyday speech Brazilians often prefer the active voice or the impersonal *se*: *Vendem-se vestidos* / *Eles vendem vestidos*.

### Resumo em inglês

- **Fiscal adjustment bill passes the Chamber:** On Monday night, Brazil's Chamber of Deputies approved the main text of the government's fiscal adjustment bill (PLP 299/2026) by 353 votes to 128, with 5 abstentions; as a complementary law it needed at least 257 of the 513 votes. The rapporteur, Mauro Benevides Filho, kept the Senate's text unchanged so it would not return to the Senate, and it now goes to the president to be signed. Among other measures, it lowers the maximum real growth of the spending limit in years of spending restraint from 2.5% to 2%, lets the government limit companies' use of tax credits, and excludes up to R$10.5 billion of this year's diesel subsidy from the 2026 fiscal target. The government called the measures necessary to control the 2027 budget; the opposition voted against, arguing it burdens the productive sector.
- **National Science and Technology Week:** The 23rd Semana Nacional de Ciência e Tecnologia, organized by the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation, runs from today until Sunday 29 November on the lawn of the Esplanada dos Ministérios in Brasília, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., with free entry. This year's theme, "Ciência Delas," celebrates women researchers and encourages girls to pursue science careers, with exhibitions, workshops, robotics and astronomy. Events also take place in other states, such as Bahia, where Salvador hosted activities in October.
- **Wedding dresses made in prison:** In Chapecó, in western Santa Catarina, eight inmates at the local penitentiary make wedding, debutante and haute-couture dresses through a partnership between the state justice department and the company Lua Azul. About 70 dresses a month are sold across Brazil, and the state's prison-work programme generated R$29 million in 2024.
